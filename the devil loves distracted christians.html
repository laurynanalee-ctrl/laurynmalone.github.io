The Devil Doesn't Fear Distracted Christians

June 2026

The devil does not fear Christians who are busy.

He fears Christians who abide.

That is why he loves distracted ones.

One of the enemy's greatest victories is not always convincing people that God does not exist. Sometimes, it is convincing Christians that they can know about God without truly walking with Him. Satan has never needed to destroy the Church from the outside if he can quietly weaken it from within. He does not always attack our faith head-on. More often, he simply redirects our attention until our affection slowly follows.

The frightening part is that distraction rarely feels dangerous.

Most of us do not wake up one morning and decide to stop pursuing Jesus. We do not suddenly abandon our convictions, throw away our Bibles, or walk away from church. Instead, we slowly fill every quiet moment with something else. We become busy. We become entertained. We become consumed by work, family, hobbies, social media, politics, sports, opinions, responsibilities, and even ministry itself.

None of those things are necessarily sinful. Many of them are good gifts from God. But good things become dangerous when they begin taking the place that belongs to God alone.

“The enemy isn't after your belief as much as he's after your attention. Because whatever has your attention will eventually shape your affection.”

When was the last time you drifted without even realizing it? Not because you chose open rebellion, but because your attention slowly wandered somewhere else. Could it be that one of the greatest threats to your relationship with Christ is not hatred toward God, but distraction from Him?

Scripture has always warned us about this. When Jesus told the Parable of the Sower, He described seed that began growing but was eventually choked by life's worries, riches, and pleasures. The plant was not uprooted by persecution. It was not destroyed by atheism. It was crowded out. It was suffocated. It never stopped growing because it lacked potential. It stopped because something else occupied the space where fruit was meant to grow.

“The seed that fell among thorns stands for those who hear, but as they go on their way they are choked by life's worries, riches and pleasures, and they do not mature.”
— Luke 8:14 (NIV)

That verse should make us pause. Jesus was not only warning about obviously sinful things. He mentioned worries. Riches. Pleasures. Ordinary things. Everyday things. Things that can look normal from the outside while quietly choking spiritual life on the inside.

What is growing in your heart right now? More peace? More love for Christ? More hunger for His Word? More patience, gentleness, and obedience? Or have the worries, responsibilities, and constant noise of life quietly crowded out the One who deserves first place?

I wonder how many Christians today are not being led away by obvious rebellion, but by constant distraction. We spend hours consuming news, podcasts, videos, reels, comments, and opinions, yet struggle to spend fifteen uninterrupted minutes in God's Word. We know what everyone else thinks about the latest controversy, but we have not stopped long enough to ask what Christ has already said. We feel informed, but we are spiritually malnourished.

What consumes more of your thoughts each day—the presence of God or the problems of the world? If someone could read your thoughts instead of your social media posts, what would they conclude you treasure most?

The enemy understands something we often overlook: whatever consistently captures your attention will eventually shape your heart. That is why Jesus said the greatest commandment is to love God with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength. Your mind matters because your attention matters. What you continually fix your thoughts on will slowly become what you trust, what you desire, and ultimately what you worship.

“Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind and with all your strength.”
— Mark 12:30 (NIV)

This is why distraction is such an effective weapon. It rarely announces itself as idolatry. It disguises itself as productivity. It convinces us that because our calendars are full, our lives must be fruitful. It tells us that because we are serving at church, listening to worship music, or sharing Bible verses online, our relationship with Jesus must be healthy.

Yet it is entirely possible to spend your life doing things for Christ while neglecting to spend time with Him.

You can spend your entire life doing things for Jesus while quietly neglecting to be with Jesus.

Martha understood this better than most. While Mary sat at Jesus' feet listening to His teaching, Martha was busy serving. Jesus did not rebuke her because service was wrong. He gently corrected her because distraction had stolen what mattered most.

“Martha, Martha,” the Lord answered, “you are worried and upset about many things, but few things are needed—or indeed only one.”
— Luke 10:41-42 (NIV)

Martha was in the same house as Jesus. She was preparing a meal for Jesus. She was serving Jesus. And somehow, she was still missing Jesus Himself.

That story should stop us in our tracks.

How many times have we mistaken being busy for being faithful? How many times have we excused spiritual emptiness because our schedules looked productive? Could it be that we have become so occupied doing things for Jesus that we have forgotten how to simply sit with Him?

We plan Bible studies but forget to study the Bible for ourselves. We lead worship but neglect private worship. We teach our children about Jesus while rarely lingering in His presence ourselves. We volunteer, organize, serve, and pour out until our calendars are full, all while our souls quietly grow empty.

Perhaps that is why Jesus' words to the church in Ephesus are so sobering. This was a church known for hard work, perseverance, doctrinal purity, and endurance. By every outward measure, they were successful. Yet Jesus looked beyond everything they were doing and said they had forsaken the love they had at first.

“Yet I hold this against you: You have forsaken the love you had at first.”
— Revelation 2:4 (NIV)

They had not abandoned truth. They had abandoned intimacy. They were still doing the right things, but they were no longer doing them from a heart captivated by Christ.

That verse terrifies me—not because it was written to unbelievers, but because it was written to a church that looked healthy. What if Jesus evaluated our lives today? Would He be impressed by everything we are doing, or would He quietly ask, “But where is the love you once had for Me?”

We do not usually lose our first love in a moment. We lose it one distraction at a time.

I cannot help but wonder if that describes many of us today.

We have become so accustomed to constant stimulation that silence feels uncomfortable. We instinctively reach for our phones whenever there is a spare moment. Waiting in line, sitting in the car, walking through the grocery store, even lying in bed before we fall asleep—we fill every empty space with noise. But what if those moments were invitations instead of interruptions? What if God wanted to meet us there, but we were too distracted to notice?

“Be still, and know that I am God.”
— Psalm 46:10 (NIV)

Stillness is difficult because it exposes where our hearts really are. In silence, we cannot hide behind productivity or entertainment. We come face to face with God—and often with ourselves. Maybe that's why we avoid it. We have become so accustomed to constant noise that we no longer recognize the sound of His voice.

When was the last time you sat in silence long enough for God to interrupt your thoughts? Or has every quiet moment become another opportunity to scroll, answer, consume, or stay busy?

The devil does not mind if your Bible sits on your coffee table as long as it stays closed. He does not mind if you attend church every Sunday as long as church becomes the closest you get to Jesus all week. He does not mind if your social media bio says "Christian" while everything else disciples your heart more than Christ does.

Because if he can keep your eyes fixed everywhere except Jesus, he knows your love for Jesus will eventually grow cold.

“The devil doesn't need to make you hate Jesus. He only needs to make you too distracted to hear Him.”

Jesus never invited us to simply work harder. He never measured spiritual maturity by how full our calendars were or how many ministries we served in. Again and again, His invitation was beautifully simple: Follow Me. Abide in Me. Remain in Me.

“Remain in me, as I also remain in you... No branch can bear fruit by itself; it must remain in the vine... apart from me you can do nothing.”
— John 15:4-5 (NIV)

Notice that Jesus doesn't tell us to produce fruit. He tells us to remain. Fruit is the evidence of abiding—it is never the substitute for it.

Maybe that's why so many Christians feel exhausted. We've been trying to manufacture fruit instead of remaining connected to the Vine. We've mistaken activity for intimacy, knowledge for transformation, and proximity to Christian things for closeness to Christ.

The enemy cannot steal your salvation, but he will gladly steal your attention—because he knows your attention always precedes your affection.

Maybe today isn't about deleting every app from your phone or abandoning every responsibility on your calendar. Maybe it begins with one honest question.

If Jesus had as much of my attention as everything else competing for it... how different would my relationship with Him look?

That question has lingered in my own heart this week.

Not because I think the answer is easy, but because I know how subtly distraction creeps in. It doesn't announce itself. It doesn't wave a red flag. It simply asks for one more minute, one more scroll, one more notification, one more headline, one more distraction—until the voice we've become most familiar with is no longer the Shepherd's.

Church attendance will never replace communion with Christ. Christian podcasts will never replace His Word. Worship music will never replace worship. Serving Him will never replace sitting with Him.

And perhaps the most sobering question isn't whether we're following Jesus.

It's whether we've become so distracted that we no longer notice when we've stopped walking closely with Him.

My prayer is not that we become less busy.

My prayer is that we become so captivated by Christ that every distraction begins to lose its grip.

“Search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. See if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.”
— Psalm 139:23-24 (NIV)
