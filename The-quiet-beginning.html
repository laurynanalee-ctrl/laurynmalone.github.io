The Quiet Beginning

July 2026

Tomorrow I start a new job.

There's something humbling about beginning again.

Not because I haven't worked hard before or because I've forgotten what I know, but because every new season reminds me how little control I've actually had over the story God has been writing all along.

If you had asked me a few years ago what my career would look like, I don't think I could have imagined this chapter.

And yet, looking back, I can see the threads.

Every position.
Every closed door.
Every disappointment.
Every unexpected opportunity.
Every season that felt like God was doing nothing.

None of it was wasted.

We spend so much of our lives praying for open doors, but when God answers, we often walk through them terrified we'll somehow ruin His plan.

As if the God who ordained the beginning of the story suddenly surrendered the ending to us.

Maybe that's because we secretly believe our future is held together more by our performance than by His providence.

That's a frightening way to live.

We often think of a new season as a test of our ability, but maybe it's really a test of our theology.

Do I believe this door opened because I'm capable?

Or because God is sovereign?

Those are two very different foundations.

One produces anxiety because everything depends on me. Every mistake becomes evidence that I don't belong.

The other produces humility because it reminds me that God has never called people on the basis of their sufficiency. He calls them according to His purpose.

Moses had excuses.
Jeremiah thought he was too young.
Gideon believed he was the weakest.
Peter was impulsive.
Paul called himself the worst of sinners.

God has never had a shortage of imperfect people. He's only ever worked through them.

Maybe that's because His glory has never been displayed through human competence, but through human dependence.

I've realized that every new beginning requires the same things: the willingness to ask questions, the humility to admit I don't know everything, and the faith to believe God isn't surprised by that.

Maybe we've mistaken confidence in ourselves for maturity.

Biblical maturity has never been believing you're capable.

It's believing Christ is.

Here's the uncomfortable truth I've been wrestling with:

Sometimes what I call humility is really just unbelief wearing church clothes.

“I could never do that.”
“I'm not ready.”
“I'm not qualified.”

Those statements sound humble on the surface, but if God has called us, aren't we ultimately questioning His wisdom more than our ability?

How often do we disguise distrust as discernment?

How often do we baptize fear and call it wisdom?

The Christian life has never been about proving ourselves worthy of God's calling. It's about faithfully stewarding what He's already entrusted to us.

So tomorrow I'll have new routines to learn, names to remember, questions to ask, and probably a few mistakes to make.

That's okay.

Because my confidence isn't in having everything figured out.

It's in the One who goes before me.

The question has never really been whether I'm enough. The question is whether Christ is enough.

He is.

The God who opens doors is the same God who walks with His people through them. He doesn't call us because we've arrived. He calls us because He intends to sanctify us along the way.

Maybe that's what new beginnings really are.

Not opportunities to prove ourselves.

But opportunities to discover, once again, that we've never been the one holding everything together.

“The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it.”
— 1 Thessalonians 5:24 (NIV)

So here's to fresh notebooks, new passwords, unfamiliar rhythms, and another opportunity to watch God's faithfulness meet me in ordinary obedience.

Not because I know exactly what this season will hold.

But because I know the One who does.

He has been faithful.

He will be faithful.

And that's enough.
