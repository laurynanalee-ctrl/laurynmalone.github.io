This House Will Never Be a Museum

August 2026

If you are comparing my home to yours, you will probably be disappointed.

There are fingerprints on my windows, crumbs under the kitchen table that I swear I just swept, little shoes scattered by the door, and toys in places toys definitely do not belong. There are blankets dragged from bedrooms to the living room, cups abandoned on random surfaces, and probably at least one mystery object shoved underneath my couch right now.

I love a clean house. I really do. I love when the counters are cleared, the floors are vacuumed, and everything is in its place. But somewhere along the way, I realized I never want my kids to confuse a clean house with a safe home.

Because this house will never be a museum.

If your standard for a good home is one that looks untouched, mine will probably disappoint you. But I don't want my boys growing up feeling like visitors in their own home. I don't want them worried about touching the wrong thing, sitting on the wrong cushion, making too much noise, spilling something, or leaving evidence that they were here.

They live here. And I want them to live here.

I want blanket forts and couch-cushion mountains. I want pictures taped to the refrigerator and random rocks brought inside because apparently that one is really cool, Mama. I want kitchen dance parties, belly laughs, bedtime conversations that go way past bedtime, and three little boys who know they don't have to make themselves smaller just to keep the house looking pretty.

Because the world is going to give them plenty of places where they feel like they have to perform. Home should not be one of them.

The world will tell them when they're too loud, too emotional, too messy, too sensitive, too much, or somehow not enough. I don't want the walls of their own home repeating those same messages. I want home to be the place where they can come through the door and exhale.

I want them to know they can tell me when they messed up. When someone hurt them. When they're scared. When they made a terrible decision. When they're questioning something. When they're carrying something they don't know how to explain.

I want home to be the safest place in the world to tell the truth.

That doesn't mean there aren't rules here. My boys will have chores. They'll learn responsibility and respect for the things God has given us. There will be correction and consequences, because biblical love isn't the absence of either.

But correction and safety can exist in the same room. Boundaries and belonging can exist in the same home.

“There is no fear in love. But perfect love drives out fear.”

— 1 John 4:18 (NIV)

I never want fear of me to keep my children from running to me.

And honestly, that matters more to me than whether someone walks into my house and notices the mess.

They might notice the toys before they notice the laughter. They might notice the crumbs before they notice three little boys gathered around the kitchen table. They might notice the clutter before they notice the artwork, half-finished projects, imaginary worlds, and evidence of childhood happening everywhere.

That's okay.

If we're going to compare homes, let's compare the things that actually matter.

Not my countertops to yours, but whether the people sitting around them feel safe. Not my floors to yours, but whether the little feet running across them know they belong. Not the laundry, the toy bins, or how perfectly staged the living room looks, but how free the people inside of it feel.

Because there is a difference between a beautiful house and a safe home.

And if I have to choose, I'm choosing safe every single time.

“Children are a heritage from the LORD, offspring a reward from him.”

— Psalm 127:3 (NIV)

Sometimes I think we say we believe children are a blessing and then get frustrated by every visible sign that they live with us.

The fingerprints. The noise. The toys. The cups. The endless “Mama, look at this.”

But maybe the evidence of children living in our homes isn't always an interruption to the blessing. Sometimes it is the blessing.

One day, those shoes won't be piled by my door anymore. One day, I won't wipe tiny fingerprints off the glass. Nobody will dump an entire basket of toys onto the floor five minutes after I cleaned it. My couch cushions will probably stay exactly where I put them.

And I have a feeling the house will be a whole lot cleaner.

I'm not convinced it will feel nearly as full.

So no, I'm not trying to create a museum. Museums are beautiful, but you're supposed to whisper. You're supposed to keep your hands to yourself, stay behind the line, and look without disturbing anything.

That's not what I want childhood to feel like.

I want my boys to disturb things. I want them to take up space. I want them to know this home belongs to them, too.

More than anything, I want our home to give them a tiny glimpse of the heart of their Heavenly Father. Jesus says in Matthew 11:28:

“Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.”

— Matthew 11:28 (NIV)

That's what I want home to feel like. Come tired. Come frustrated. Come when you've had a terrible day. Come when you messed up. Come when someone hurt you. Come when you're carrying something too heavy for you.

Come home and rest.

I think about the prodigal son. He came home knowing exactly how badly he had failed, expecting to be treated like a servant. But Jesus tells us that while he was still a long way off, his father saw him, was filled with compassion, and ran toward him.

That doesn't mean his choices didn't matter. It means his failure didn't erase his place in the family.

That's the kind of safety I want to build here. A home where correction exists, but shame doesn't get the final word. A home where we apologize, forgive, and try again. A home where grace isn't just something we talk about at church, but something my boys experience around the dinner table and in the middle of ordinary Tuesday afternoons.

And I know I won't get this right every time.

There will be days when the mess gets on my nerves. Days when I'm overstimulated and somebody spilling Goldfish crackers across the freshly vacuumed floor feels significantly more serious than it actually is. I'll lose my patience sometimes. I'll have to apologize sometimes.

Maybe that's part of making home safe, too—letting my children see that their mom doesn't expect perfection because she doesn't possess it either.

So if you walk into my house and compare it to yours, you may see everything that needs to be cleaned.

I hope I see something different.

I hope I see boys who feel comfortable enough to laugh loudly and play hard. Children who aren't terrified of spilling something because they know mistakes can be cleaned up. Little hearts that know there is absolutely nothing they could tell me that would make me stop loving them.

“Whoever fears the LORD has a secure fortress, and for their children it will be a refuge.”

— Proverbs 14:26 (NIV)

A refuge. That's what I want.

Not a showroom. Not a performance. Not a house that looks perfect to everyone outside of it while the people inside feel like they're walking on eggshells.

So yes, you might have cleaner floors than me. You might have better organization, less clutter on your counters, and a couch you don't have to check for toys before sitting down.

And that's okay.

I'm not trying to win the clean-house competition.

I'm trying to raise boys who know what safety feels like.

I want them to grow up knowing that home is where they can be loud, messy, honest, emotional, wrong, forgiven, corrected, loved, and completely themselves. And someday, when they're grown and the world has given them plenty of reasons to guard themselves, I hope they remember one place where they never had to.

A house that wasn't perfect. A house that was definitely lived in. A house that probably had crumbs under the table.

But a house where they always knew they belonged.

This home will never be a museum. It will be a refuge.

And I'll choose that over picture-perfect every single time.
