If They Met Us Before They Met Jesus

A quote crossed my screen recently that stopped me in my tracks.

Not because it was controversial. Not because it was harsh. But because it exposed something I don't think we talk about enough.

"If our churches are doing a good job representing Jesus, people should feel welcomed there. If they don't, then maybe we should ask ourselves if we are doing a good job representing Jesus."

The longer I sat with those words, the more uncomfortable they became.

As Christians, we spend a lot of time talking about reaching people for Jesus. We invest in ministries, worship services, Bible studies, outreach events, and discipleship programs. We celebrate salvations and pray for prodigals to come home.

All of those things matter.

But this quote made me wonder if we're sometimes overlooking something much simpler.

What does it actually feel like to be around us?

Not when we're on stage. Not when we're serving in a ministry role. Not when we're posting Scripture on social media.

Just us.

Our conversations. Our attitudes. Our willingness to notice people. Our ability to make room for someone who doesn't look, think, vote, dress, worship, or struggle the same way we do.

Because for many people, the first impression they have of Jesus won't come from opening a Bible.

It will come from encountering someone who claims to follow Him.

That's a sobering thought.

If Jesus sent the lonely, the grieving, the addicted, the doubting, and the overlooked to our church, would they feel welcomed by us the same way they would have felt welcomed by Him?

That question has lingered in my heart.

Because it isn't really a question about church growth. It isn't a question about programming. It isn't even a question about theology.

It's a question about our hearts.

The truth is, it is possible to know Scripture and still miss people. It is possible to defend truth while neglecting compassion. It is possible to talk about grace while being difficult to approach.

"Dear children, let us not love with words or speech but with actions and in truth."
— 1 John 3:18 (NIV)

John reminds us that love was never meant to be something we simply talk about. It was meant to be something people experience.

It is a terrifying thought that someone could spend years hearing about the love of Jesus while never feeling it from His people.

Not because believers are intentionally unkind. Not because churches don't care. But because busyness, comfort, routine, and familiarity can slowly dull our awareness of the people around us.

We forget what it felt like to be the new person. The person standing alone. The person carrying grief they haven't told anyone about. The person whose marriage is struggling. The person battling addiction. The person silently wondering if anyone notices they're there.

The beautiful thing about the Gospel is that every one of us came to Christ needing grace.

None of us earned our place in God's family. None of us cleaned ourselves up first. None of us arrived worthy.

We were welcomed by Jesus long before we deserved it.

And because of that, we have the opportunity—and the responsibility—to extend that same welcome to others.

Maybe the question isn't whether our churches have room for more people. Maybe the question is whether our hearts do.

Long after people forget our sermons, our programs, our preferences, and our traditions, they will remember how we made them feel.

My prayer is that when people encounter God's people, they catch a glimpse of God's heart.

And if they meet us before they meet Jesus, I pray they recognize Him anyway.
