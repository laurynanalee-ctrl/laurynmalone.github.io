Don’t Waste What God Brought You Through

September 2026

I saw something recently about our hardest seasons becoming our greatest testimonies, and it got me thinking.

We love the idea of having a testimony, but none of us wants to live through the part that creates one.

We want resurrection without the grave.
We want freedom without the wilderness.
We want to say God carried us without ever reaching the place where He is all we have left to hold on to.

But a testimony is not formed in comfort. It is formed in the places where our own strength fails and the faithfulness of God becomes the only explanation for why we are still standing.

When the Israelites crossed the Jordan River, God told Joshua to have twelve men pick up twelve stones from the middle of the river.

They were to build a memorial so that when their children asked what those stones meant, they could tell them what God had done.

“He did this so that all the peoples of the earth might know that the hand of the Lord is powerful and so that you might always fear the Lord your God.”

Joshua 4:24, NIV

Those stones were not decorations.

They were evidence.

They reminded the Israelites that the ground beneath their feet had once been covered by something they could not cross on their own.

We all want to leave the river behind, but God told them to carry something out of it.

Maybe we should stop being so quick to bury every reminder of what we have been through.

We hide the anxious seasons because we think strong Christians should have trusted God better.

We stay quiet about the depression because we are afraid someone will question our faith.

We cover the scars because we would rather people see the polished version of us.

But what if the thing you are trying to hide is the very stone God wants you to carry?

Not so everyone can admire your strength, but so they can see His.

Your testimony was never meant to make you look impressive. It is meant to make the faithfulness of God impossible to ignore.

That means we have to stop rewriting the story as though we saved ourselves.

We did not climb out because we suddenly became stronger. We did not survive because we finally got everything right.

We are here because the grace of God held us together when we could not hold ourselves together.

Maybe God did not remove the struggle as quickly as you wanted. Maybe the prayer was not answered the way you expected. Maybe you still carry evidence that the season happened.

So did the Israelites.

The stones did not erase the river. They testified that God made a way through it.

The enemy would love for you to remember only the pain and forget the Provider.

He wants you ashamed of the nights you barely made it because he knows what might happen when you begin telling people Who met you there.

Someone else will realize they are not alone.
Someone else will believe God can meet them too.
Someone else will find the courage to keep walking.

So tell the truth about the river.

Tell them how afraid you were. Tell them about the prayers that were barely more than tears. Tell them that you loved Jesus and still struggled.

Then tell them that He did not leave.

Do not make an idol out of the pain, but do not waste it either.

Carry the stone.

Let your life point back to the place where the water once looked impossible to cross and say:

“That is where God made a way for me.”
