When Both Doors Are Good (and I Still Don’t Know Which One to Walk Through)

April 2026

I didn’t expect choosing between two good things to feel this heavy. I think I always imagined that when something was “right,” it would come with this clear sense of peace, like everything would just fall into place and I would know without questioning it.

But that’s not where I find myself right now.

Instead, I’m standing in front of two open doors, and both of them make sense. Both of them feel like good opportunities. Both of them could lead to something meaningful, and that’s exactly what’s making this so hard.

One option feels steady and familiar, the kind of place where I can already picture what my days would look like. The other feels a little more unknown, like it might stretch me in ways I can’t fully see yet, and if I’m honest, that part makes me hesitate.

I’ve found myself going back and forth, trying to measure something that doesn’t really fit neatly into a list. I’ve made the pros and cons. I’ve replayed conversations. I’ve tried to imagine the future like I could somehow predict which version of my life would be better.

And the more I’ve done that, the more I’ve realized this isn’t just about a job. Because moments like this have a way of bringing other things to the surface—the need to get it right, the pressure to not waste an opportunity, the urge to hold onto control when the outcome feels important. It’s strange how quickly a simple decision can start to feel like it carries more weight than it should.

That question has been sitting underneath all of this: what if I choose wrong? Not wrong in the sense of something bad, but wrong in the sense of missing the best thing. And the more I’ve sat with that, the more I’ve started to notice how much of that thinking assumes everything depends on me getting this exactly right.

But when I bring that back to Scripture, it starts to shift. Proverbs 3:5–6 tells us to trust in the Lord with all our heart and not lean on our own understanding, and I’ve realized how easy it is to love that verse in theory and how much harder it is to live it when both choices make sense.

Because in moments like this, trusting God doesn’t look like having a clear answer. It looks like releasing the need to have one before moving.

So maybe the question isn’t actually, “What if I choose wrong?” Maybe the better question is, “What does it look like to trust God here?”.

Where are you trying to have everything figured out before you take a step? Where have you been going in circles, trying to make the perfect choice instead of surrendering the decision altogether? Is there something in your life right now that feels like two open doors, and instead of moving forward, you’ve just been standing there, afraid to choose?

I don’t have a neat answer for this yet. I’m still in it. Still praying, still asking for wisdom, still learning how to sit in that tension without rushing to fix it just to feel settled again. But I am starting to see that God isn’t waiting for me on the other side of one specific choice.

He’s with me in the choosing, in the uncertainty, and in whatever comes next.

And the same is true for you.

He’s not limited by your decision, your timing, or your ability to get it exactly right. He’s not asking you to predict the future. He’s asking you to trust Him with it.

So maybe this isn’t about finding the one perfect path that guarantees everything will unfold exactly how you hope. Maybe it’s about walking forward with a surrendered heart, trusting that wherever you go, He’s already there, already working, already leading.

Because this was never really just about a job.

It’s about trust.

And maybe for you… it is too.
