Measuring My Worth by My Usefulness

April 2026

I thought obedience would feel peaceful.
I didn’t expect it to feel so much like being forgotten.

Lately, I’ve been learning that purpose can look very different depending on the season you’re in.

There are some seasons of life where everything feels loud and full. You feel needed. Involved. Constantly moving. People call. People ask for help. Your schedule stays packed, your mind stays busy, and honestly, it can feel good to be needed.

There’s something validating about being the person people rely on. The person trusted with responsibility. The person included in conversations, plans, decisions, and last-minute problem solving. You start to feel connected to the movement of everything around you.

And without even realizing it, sometimes you start measuring your value by your usefulness.

Then there are seasons where God starts pulling you back a little.

Not necessarily because you did something wrong. Not because the things you were doing were bad. But because sometimes God shifts our focus before He explains why.

And if I’m being honest, that shift has been harder for me than I expected.

Because when you’ve spent so much time pouring into certain places, certain people, and certain routines, stepping back can feel strange. Watching life continue without your constant involvement can make you quietly question where you fit now.

I think a lot of us struggle with that, even if we don’t say it out loud.

We live in a culture that praises hustle, visibility, productivity, and constantly being “on.” Somewhere along the way, many of us started connecting our worth to how much we do. How involved we are. How needed we feel.

And the scary part is, sometimes we disguise it as purpose.

We tell ourselves we’re “just serving.” We say yes to everything because we care. We keep stretching ourselves thin because we want to help people. But eventually, if we’re not careful, we start building identity in places God only intended for assignment.

There’s a difference between being called somewhere and becoming consumed by it.

I don’t think God was ever asking us to carry every single thing all the time.

“Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.”
Matthew 11:28

Not more pressure.
Not more striving.
Rest.

And honestly? I think some of us feel uncomfortable with rest because rest forces us to confront who we are when we’re no longer constantly producing.

That part has challenged me deeply.

Because when things get quieter, you notice what you were using to validate yourself. You notice how easy it is to feel forgotten when you’re no longer in the middle of everything. You notice how much identity can accidentally get tied to positions, routines, recognition, or being the dependable one.

But lately, God has been reminding me that purpose was never supposed to be rooted in a position.

Some of the most meaningful seasons in Scripture happened far away from crowds and recognition. David was anointed king and then sent back to the field. Moses spent years in the wilderness before stepping into leadership. Even Jesus often withdrew from people to spend quiet time with the Father.

Hidden seasons were never wasted seasons.

“Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.”
Colossians 3:23

Not whatever gets noticed.

Not whatever gets applause.

Not whatever keeps you relevant.

Whatever you do.

That means purpose still exists in the quiet seasons too.

Sometimes purpose looks like leading. Sometimes it looks like resting. Sometimes it looks like building. Other times it looks like releasing.

And maybe maturity is learning not to idolize one season over another.

Maybe the quiet season isn’t punishment. Maybe it’s preparation. Maybe it’s protection. Maybe it’s God teaching us that we were never meant to find our identity in being constantly needed by people.

Because people will always move on eventually.

Roles will change.
Seasons will shift.
Doors will open and close.
Someone else will eventually step into places we once held.

That’s life.

But God remains constant through all of it.

And if our identity is built on Him instead of our usefulness, we won’t completely fall apart every time a season changes.

I’m realizing that obedience does not always feel exciting. Sometimes it feels unfamiliar. Sometimes it feels quieter than we expected. Sometimes it feels like trusting God enough to let go of where we were comfortable so He can grow something deeper in us.

And maybe that’s where some of you are right now too.

Maybe God is asking you to slow down.
Maybe He’s asking you to release something.
Maybe He’s teaching you how to serve without striving.
Maybe He’s reminding you that your worth is not attached to your productivity.

If that’s you, I hope you hear this clearly:

You do not have to constantly prove your value to still be valuable.

God is not more pleased with you when you’re exhausted, overextended, and trying to carry everything at once. Faithfulness matters more than visibility ever will.

So if you’ve been struggling with feeling overlooked, replaceable, disconnected, or unsure of where you fit in this season, maybe this is your reminder too:

Your purpose is not limited to the places people see you most.

God still works in quiet seasons.
God still calls people in hidden places.
And sometimes the holiest thing we can do is trust Him enough to embrace the season we’re in instead of constantly chasing the one we left behind.
