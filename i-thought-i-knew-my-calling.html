I Thought I Knew My Calling

September 2026

For a long time, I thought finding your calling was the hard part.

I thought there would be this moment when everything clicked. You'd finally understand what God put you here to do, step into it, and suddenly all the uncertainty would disappear.

I thought once I knew my purpose, I'd feel confident in it.

But lately, I don't feel very confident at all.

If you asked me what I'm called to do, I could probably give you a few answers.

And then immediately question every single one of them.

I think I'm called to worship.

But I'm not sure.

I think I'm called to teach.

But I don't always feel confident enough.

I think I'm called to write.

But sometimes I'm so worried about what people will think that I'd rather keep everything between me and God.

And then there's the one I really don't want to talk about.

Speaking.

There have been moments when I've felt the Holy Spirit whispering to my heart:

"One day, you're going to speak."

And I wish I could tell you my response was some beautiful, surrendered, "Here I am, Lord. Send me."

It wasn't.

It was more like:

Absolutely not.

The thought genuinely makes me want to throw up.

Because I can worship.

Give me a microphone and a worship song and, somehow, I can do that. I'm still nervous. I still question whether I'm good enough to be standing there. But there's something about worship that lets me disappear into what I'm singing.

It's not really about me.

So I can do it scared.

I can teach.

Put me in front of my students with a lesson to teach and I'll figure it out. But even there, I question myself constantly.

Am I actually good at this?

Am I making a difference?

Am I really the teacher they need?

Was this actually something God called me to, or was it just something I happened to be good enough at?

And then there's writing.

Writing feels different.

Writing lets me say the things I can't always say out loud.

Give me a blank page and enough time to overthink every sentence before I hit publish, and I'll tell you about the things God is teaching me, the things I'm wrestling with, the things that hurt, and the things I don't completely understand yet.

But even writing scares me.

Because eventually, I have to hit publish.

And once I do, I can't control how people receive it.

Someone might misunderstand me. Someone might disagree. Someone might judge my theology. Someone might think I'm being dramatic. Someone might wonder why I think anyone needs to hear what I have to say in the first place.

And if I'm being completely transparent, sometimes that fear is enough to make me want to stay quiet.

But speaking feels different.

Speaking feels like there would be nowhere to hide.

Writing gives me time to think.

Worship gives me lyrics to sing.

Teaching gives me something to teach.

But standing in front of a room with nothing to hide behind and telling people what God has put on my heart?

That feels entirely too vulnerable.

And maybe that's why it scares me so much.

I don't know exactly what those words mean yet. I don't know if "speak" means standing on a stage, sharing my testimony, teaching Scripture, talking to a small room of women, or something I haven't even imagined.

I'm not going to pretend I have that part figured out.

I just know I've felt the nudge.

And I've also felt the fear immediately answer it.

Maybe I haven't lost my calling. Maybe I'm scared of it.

There's a difference.

Because when I really look at the things I question, I notice something.

I love worship.

I love teaching.

I love writing.

The questioning usually gets louder when I start looking at myself.

I'm not talented enough.

I'm not confident enough.

I don't know enough.

Someone else could do it better.

What will people think?

And suddenly, "Am I called to this?" gets tangled up with "Am I good enough for this?"

Those aren't the same question.

I'm starting to realize that I have spent a lot of time trying to determine my calling based on how qualified I feel to carry it.

And maybe that's why I've been so confused.

Because I don't feel qualified.

I don't feel particularly brave.

I definitely don't feel ready to stand on a stage and speak.

And there are still days when I'd much rather God hand whatever He's doing in my life to someone a little more confident.

Someone who doesn't overthink everything.

Someone who doesn't worry so much about being judged.

Someone who hears, "One day, you're going to speak," and thinks, "Wow, that's exciting!"

Instead of:

Please don't say that again because I might actually be sick.

But I'm not the first person to feel unqualified.

The Bible is full of people whose first response to God's calling wasn't confidence.

Moses essentially asked, Who am I to do this? And when that wasn't enough, he reminded God that he wasn't a good speaker.

Jeremiah's response was that he didn't know how to speak.

Gideon wanted to know how God could possibly use someone like him.

And God didn't respond by convincing them they were secretly amazing.

He reminded them that He would be with them.

Maybe that's the part I've been missing.

I've been asking God to make me confident in myself when maybe He's teaching me to be confident in Him.

That doesn't mean every uncomfortable thing is automatically a calling.

And I don't want to manufacture a calling just because I think I've heard something.

I want to pray about it. I want to test it against Scripture. I want to listen. I want to give God room to confirm, redirect, or completely change what I think He's doing.

But I also don't want fear to make the decision.

I don't want to call something "not my calling" just because it scares me.

That's the tension I'm sitting in right now.

I'm still learning the difference between God's redirection and my own insecurity.

Because sometimes "Maybe God isn't calling me to this" sounds a whole lot more spiritual than admitting:

I'm scared I'm not good enough.

I'm scared I'll fail.

I'm scared I'll embarrass myself.

I'm scared people will judge me.

I'm scared I'll step out believing God told me to do something and discover that I heard Him wrong.

I'm scared of being seen trying.

And maybe that's why this season feels so confusing.

I want God's will for my life.

I really do.

I just wish His will always lined up perfectly with the places where I already felt confident.

But maybe faithfulness isn't waiting until I'm completely sure of myself.

Maybe it's being willing to say:

"God, I'm not sure about me. But I'm sure about You."

So where does that leave me?

I still don't have a neat answer for what I'm called to do.

I think worship is part of it.

I think teaching is part of it.

I can't seem to get away from writing, no matter how nervous hitting "publish" makes me.

And as for speaking...

I'm still hoping God meant somebody else.

But I'm trying not to slam the door shut just because I'm scared of what's on the other side.

Instead, my prayer has become something a little different:

God, if You called me to it, give me the courage to keep showing up.

If You didn't, give me the wisdom to let it go.

Help me recognize the difference between Your redirection and my insecurity.

And while I'm figuring it out, don't let fear make the decision for me.

Maybe I don't need to have my entire purpose figured out right now.

Maybe I don't need a title for my calling.

Maybe I don't even need to feel confident yet.

Maybe, for now, I just need enough faith for the next yes.

Because I'm beginning to think calling isn't always a place where you finally arrive and say,

"This is exactly what I was made to do."

Sometimes it might look more like standing in front of God with shaking hands and saying:

"I don't know if I can do this. But if You're really asking me to, I'll trust You enough to try."
